    Rental terms

    Reserved pricing trades a spending or time commitment for a lower hourly rate than on-demand, and spot capacity costs less again because the provider can interrupt it. Compare offers within the same rental model before comparing providers.

    AWS lists its H100 p5 instance at $6.88 per GPU-hour on demand, $5.42 under a one-year Compute Savings Plan and $2.97 under a three-year EC2 Instance Savings Plan. Microsoft lists its ND H100 v5 at $12.29, falling to $7.87 with a one-year reservation and $5.40 with a three-year one. The lower rates come with longer commitments, so neither figure alone shows which offer costs less for a given workload.

    The neocloud and AI platform rate cards list H100s at $3.20 to $6.16 per GPU-hour on demand. Together AI lists $3.19 for a 91 to 180 day reservation, and Verda's published discounts bring a two-year term to about $3.07. CoreWeave advertises committed-use discounts of up to 60%, and Nebius up to 35%. Advertised discounts do not establish the prices customers negotiate in private contracts.

    Availability and price changes

    Lambda's rate card lists on-demand H100s without showing live availability, so a published rate does not guarantee that capacity is free to rent. The rates also price different units. Lambda, CoreWeave, AWS, Google Cloud and Azure quote a share of an eight-GPU instance, while Verda and Runpod price a single GPU. Check the instance size as well as the hourly rate.

    SemiAnalysis reports that one-year H100 rental contracts rose from $1.70 per GPU-hour in October 2025 to $2.35 in March 2026, an increase of 38%. Those are year-long contracts, so the series measures a different market from the hourly rates above.

    Cost per completed job

    An hourly rate becomes useful when you know how many billed hours a job needs. Compare the full instance charge, runtime, and any storage or data-transfer charges. For reserved capacity, count the hours you pay for but do not use. For spot capacity, include the work lost to interruptions.

    Consider a simplified example. At $2.50 per reserved GPU-hour, using only half the hours you pay for raises the compute cost to $5.00 per used GPU-hour. If failures also consume 8% of those used hours, the cost reaches about $5.43 per useful GPU-hour. These figures assume idle and failed hours remain billable and exclude other charges.

    About the data

    Every profile sentence and key figure links to its source, drawn from company filings, announcements and reporting through October 14, 2026. The network links a provider to a customer only when a source names the relationship, and a link shows that the relationship exists, not how large it is.
